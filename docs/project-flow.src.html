Operations Live Board · Project outline

From working demo to running system

What is already built, what is left, who we need to talk to, and roughly what it costs in money and in weeks.

A working board already exists. It takes the barcode scans that happen anyway on the picking floor and turns them into a live picture of the department — who is working where, how fast, and what is getting in their way — plus a printed handover at the end of every shift.

It runs today on invented data. Everything that remains is about replacing that invented data with the real thing: getting the scanners, the warehouse system and the plant signals to talk to it, agreeing how people may be measured, and running it on one department before the rest.

Every figure in this document is an estimate Costs are days of work at a placeholder day rate, not a quotation. Durations are elapsed weeks, including the time other teams need to answer. Both tighten sharply once the first two conversations on page 5 have happened.

The front end is not the point

The board you have seen was built with whatever front-end technology got a convincing demo fastest. That choice is deliberately disposable.

What matters sits behind it: the rules (what counts as working time, what counts as idle, what a target means) and the one door every scan comes through. Those two were designed to outlive the screens.

Rebuilding the whole visible board in a different stack — a corporate framework, a different charting library, a wall-display app — is roughly 6–10 days, and changes none of the numbers it shows. The screens are the cheapest part to replace and the easiest part to argue about; this document is about everything else.

One item needs defining before it can be costed: the central system referred to as CSD. Everything else in this plan is named, owned and estimated.

How to read the money

Days × a placeholder day rate Build effort only, at an assumed blended rate — replace the rate and every figure moves with it. The spread is not padding: it is the difference between a warehouse interface that is documented and one that has to be discovered.
Not included anywhere above Devices and licences · the server · network changes · other teams' hours · support once live. These sit with the departments that already own them.
Why stage 0 being done matters The part normally bought on trust — what it looks like, what it measures, whether anyone reads it — already exists. What remains is plumbing, agreement and proof, all far easier to estimate than invention.
What would move the total A warehouse interface needing vendor development · CSD turning out to be a system rather than a feed · every department at once rather than one first.

5 · Who speaks to whom

The conversations that actually move this forward

Most of the elapsed time on page 3 is not build time — it is the wait between asking one of these groups a question and getting an answer.

Department management

Owns the problem

  • Sets the target and what counts as a good shift
  • Chooses which leg of stations pilots first
  • Approves each stage

Weekly · already running

Warehouse system supplier

Owns the order data

  • Confirms the interface for our version and site
  • Provides a test environment
  • Names one technical contact

First conversation · blocks stage 3

Device suppliers

Own the scanners

  • Exact models and firmware in use
  • How to switch on the “could not read” signal
  • Toolkit and sample code for the wearables

Second conversation · blocks stage 2

Internal IT

Owns the route

  • A small server the scanners can reach
  • Network rule between devices and server
  • Permission to put one small app on the wearables

Early · blocks everything on the floor

Automation / controls

Owns the plant signals

  • Read-only access to equipment state
  • Which signals mean a genuine stop
  • Planned stop calendar

Before stage 4

Employee representatives

Own the consent

  • Whether names or neutral codes are shown
  • Who may see performance, and in what form
  • Signs off before anything reaches the floor

From day one · hard gate

Data protection

Owns the rules

  • What is kept and for how long
  • Lawful basis for measuring
  • Reviews the same document as above

From day one · hard gate

Shift supervisors

Own whether it is used

  • Tell us when a number looks wrong
  • Tune the thresholds against what they see
  • Decide whether the handover is worth printing

Daily during the pilot

Two hard gates, and only two No real person's name reaches a screen before the employee representatives and data protection have both signed. The board can already replace every name with a neutral code, so the pilot can run either way — but that decision is theirs, not ours.
The two conversations to have first The warehouse system supplier and the device suppliers: together they decide whether stages 2 and 3 take three weeks or eight, which is most of the spread in the total.
